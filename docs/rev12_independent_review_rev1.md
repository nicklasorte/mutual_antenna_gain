**Verdict.** The arithmetic of revision 12 is sound, and the main new explanation is real. These all recompute exactly:

- The ratio ends 28.37 to 437.5 and 21.69 to 89.24.

MATLAB reproduces the null exactly. For VCP 212 at 0.5° and 60 dBi it gives 17,570, 3,205, 115, and 9 null trials of 20,000. For VCP 34 it gives 6,131, 111, and 49. Long periodic runs do come from locks over multiples of their period. Within each period, the drift of the long runs has a median of about 1°, against 17° to 37° for the other trials. The chance that every long run falls that tight by accident is below 1e-12.

Four claims don't hold as written:

- **W1 (Medium).** "A long periodic run is regular, not heavier, exposure." The rate counts only lattice hits, so it can't exceed about 1/k of the volumes. That puts it below the VCP 34 average by construction for any k ≥ 3. Even so, 5 of the 32 long runs are above their mode's average, including a 54-volume run in which every volume is high.
- **W2 (Medium).** "22 to 440 times more common than chance." The interval covers only Monte Carlo error. The null still uses each seed's pooled share. With the trial-to-trial spread the seeds imply, the ratio ranges become about 16 to 113 (VCP 212) and 12 to 52 (VCP 34). The qualitative result holds.
- **W3 (Medium).** In "10 of the 22" and "22 of the 57 trials with a lock that tight", the lock is measured against the period of each trial's own run, which is an outcome. With uniform slips and the same periods, 12.6 and 37.0 trials would be that tight. A lock computed from the slip alone, which is what the rate tolerance gives, makes these 10 of 37 and 22 of 117.
- **W4 (Medium).** Periodic runs stop at a period of 4 volumes, so tight locks over 5 to 16 volumes go unmeasured. Seven trials have a lock within 1° over 5 to 16 volumes and a periodic run of only 4 to 17 volumes. One VCP 34 trial drifts 0.041° per 16 volumes, tighter than every trial but one, and `lock_drift_multiple_rev1` gives it 22.5°.
